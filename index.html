Find the comparable option in your plan

Look for a target-risk fund or a mix of broad Canadian, US and international equity index funds plus bonds. Match the equity percentage and geographic exposure, not the word “Growth.” A target-date fund changes its mix over time; it is not a constant-risk substitute for VGRO.

Ask HR or Sun Life for the actual fund list, total member-borne annual costs, match formula, RRSP vs DPSP structure, vesting, and transfer restrictions. A lower-cost group index option may change the answer considerably.

WEALTHSIMPLE + VANGUARD

A brokerage is not a fund.

The main comparison is Wealthsimple self-directed, buying a Canadian-listed Vanguard asset-allocation ETF in Canadian dollars. Wealthsimple advertises $0 stock/ETF trading commissions. You still pay the ETF’s ongoing fund expenses and bid–ask spreads.

All five Vanguard product pages currently show 0.17% management fee and 0.22% MER / total fund expense ratio. The calculator uses 0.22%, not 0.17%. MER is a reported, backward-looking figure; check it again before investing.

Wealthsimple managed investing is a different product: its public schedule lists 0.5% Core, 0.4% Premium, and 0.2–0.4% Generation management fees, plus applicable taxes and portfolio fund expenses. Our optional platform-cost sensitivity is fixed; it does not automatically switch wealth tiers or reproduce the managed portfolios.

Why higher equity exposure has a wider band

More equities generally means a bumpier ride. The illustration uses 2,000 paired random market paths with a constant allocation and monthly compounding. It does not use recent performance to promise a future return, and it does not capture all crashes, inflation shocks or prolonged market regimes.

How the model works—and where it stops

Equal budget, honest refunds

Every route starts with the same annual personal cash commitment and existing savings. Contributions and employer deposits are spread across month-ends. Actual tax reductions on employee RRSP contributions are reinvested across the following year; there is no instant infinite refund “gross-up.” The match is not awarded again on reinvested tax savings.

Account room is enforced

RRSP employer dollars use RRSP room when the match is a group RRSP contribution. A DPSP match creates a pension adjustment next year instead. New RRSP room is based on prior-year modeled earned income, capped at $33,810 for 2026 and the published $35,390 for 2027; later caps are inflation projections. Other pension adjustments are editable. Future TFSA room starts from the $7,000 2026 limit and is projected with inflation, rounded to $500—not asserted as future law.

Each strategy takes any match first (if selected), then fills its named account, then the other registered account. Unused room carries forward. Once both are full, money stays in visible, zero-interest cash. After employment ends, arriving tax savings go to TFSA/cash rather than claiming a deduction against no salary.

Ontario income tax

2026 federal and Ontario brackets, federal basic-personal-amount phaseout, Ontario surtaxes, the health premium, basic Ontario tax reduction, Canada employment credit, base CPP/EI credits and enhanced CPP deductions are included. Refund = estimated tax before minus tax after the employee deduction. Employer RRSP taxable benefit and matching deduction offset; there is no extra refund on the employer dollar.

We assume an Ontario employee with basic credits only. LIFT, Canada Workers Benefit, family benefits, dependants, donations, other deductions and income-tested benefits are omitted. CPP/EI are based on salary only; additional payroll charges on an employer benefit below annual maxima are not modeled. Future thresholds and credits are held constant in purchasing-power terms, including currently frozen Ontario thresholds. This is a policy assumption, not a future tax forecast.

Retirement is a reserve, not a tax return

The adjustable retirement-tax percentage is an assumed effective tax cost of eventually drawing the deferred balances—not a tax on liquidating everything at 72. CPP/OAS income, OAS recovery tax, GIS, pension splitting, senior credits, estate taxes and spending drawdowns are not modeled. The sensitivity table shows why this assumption matters.

Returns and fees

Dividends and interest are reinvested within the assumed total return. Monthly gross returns are lognormal: log-return mean = ln(1 + assumed compound return) / 12; volatility = annual log-return volatility / √12. The arithmetic mean return is therefore higher than the compound-return input. All strategies share identical market draws, isolating account and fee choices. Monte Carlo medians need not equal the steady-return line.

Percentage fees compound monthly; Sun Life’s flat fee rises with inflation. Transfers pay the entered one-time cost on each actual transfer. All non-cash investments retain the selected mix throughout. No fund-specific alpha, tracking error, currency-trading costs, foreign withholding-tax differences, liquidity restrictions or individualized advice is modeled. A 10th-percentile outcome is not a worst case; worse outcomes are possible.
