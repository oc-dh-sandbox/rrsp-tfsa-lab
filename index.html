RRSP DOLLARS ARE NOT TFSA DOLLARS

Accounting for tax later

Snapshot is at the start of age 72, before that year’s RRIF withdrawal. No living-expense withdrawals are modeled before 72. An RRSP must be converted by the end of the year you turn 71; a RRIF ordinarily requires 5.40% at 72 using your own age. DPSP money is assumed vested and transferable, not locked in.

Finding a comparable investment

Look for a target-risk fund or broad Canadian, US and international equity funds plus bonds. Match the equity percentage and geographic exposure, not the name “Growth.” Target-date funds change their mix over time and are not constant-risk substitutes for VGRO.

Ask HR for the actual fund list, total member-borne annual costs, match formula, RRSP/DPSP structure, vesting and transfer restrictions.

WEALTHSIMPLE + VANGUARD

A brokerage is not a fund.

The main comparison is Wealthsimple self-directed, buying Canadian-listed Vanguard ETFs in Canadian dollars. Wealthsimple advertises $0 stock/ETF trading commissions; ETF expenses and bid–ask spreads still exist.

All five Vanguard product pages currently show 0.17% management fee and 0.22% MER / total fund expense ratio. The calculator uses 0.22%. MER is backward-looking and can change.

Wealthsimple managed investing is a different product: 0.5% Core, 0.4% Premium and 0.2–0.4% Generation management fees, plus applicable taxes and portfolio fund expenses. The optional platform-cost sensitivity here is fixed; it does not switch wealth tiers or reproduce the managed portfolios.

About the market range

2,000 paired random paths illustrate uncertainty at a constant investment mix. Return and volatility presets are educational assumptions, not Vanguard forecasts or historical backtests. A 10th-percentile outcome is not a worst case; worse outcomes are possible.

All calculation conventions & limitations

Equal budget, honest refunds

Every route starts with the same personal cash commitment and existing savings. Employee and employer contributions are spread across month-ends. Actual tax reductions on employee RRSP contributions are reinvested across the following year. There is no instant recursive refund gross-up, borrowing or second match on reinvested tax savings.

Contribution room

Group RRSP employer dollars use current RRSP room. A DPSP match creates a pension adjustment next year instead. New RRSP room uses prior-year modeled earned income, capped at $33,810 in 2026 and the published $35,390 in 2027; later caps are inflation projections. Other pension adjustments are editable. Future TFSA room starts at the $7,000 2026 limit, grows with assumed inflation and rounds to $500—not a statement of future law.

Each route takes any selected match first, then fills its named account, then the other registered account. Unused room carries forward. Money beyond both limits is visible 0%-return cash. Once employment stops, arriving tax savings go to TFSA/cash rather than claiming a deduction against no salary.

Ontario tax

2026 federal/Ontario brackets, federal basic-personal-amount phaseout, Ontario surtaxes, health premium, basic Ontario reduction, employment credit, base CPP/EI credits and enhanced CPP deductions are included. Refund = tax before minus tax after the employee deduction. The employer RRSP taxable benefit and matching deduction offset; no extra refund is credited on the employer dollar.

This is a basic Ontario employee estimate. LIFT, Canada Workers Benefit, family benefits, dependants, donations, other deductions and income-tested benefits are omitted. CPP/EI use salary only; additional payroll charges on employer benefits below annual maxima are not modeled. Future thresholds/credits are held constant in purchasing-power terms, including currently frozen Ontario amounts. This is a policy assumption, not a forecast.

Tax in retirement

The adjustable tax percentage is a reserve for the effective cost of eventually drawing deferred balances—not a tax on liquidating everything at 72. CPP/OAS income, OAS recovery tax, GIS, pension splitting, senior credits, estate taxes and spending withdrawals are not modeled. The deeper view includes tax sensitivity.

Returns & fees

Distributions are reinvested in the total return. Monthly returns are lognormal: log-return mean = ln(1 + assumed compound return) / 12; volatility = annual log-return volatility / √12. The arithmetic mean is therefore higher than the compound input. Every route shares the same draws. Simulated medians need not equal the steady-return line.

The percentile range is pointwise, not a confidence guarantee for whole paths. Independent monthly lognormal returns omit fat tails, persistent regimes, uncertain long-run expected returns and stochastic inflation. Percentage fees compound monthly; the Sun Life flat fee grows with inflation. Actual transfers pay the entered charge. The mix stays constant; fund alpha, tracking error, currency-trading costs, foreign withholding differences and liquidity restrictions are omitted.

Your data

Calculation and simulation stay in the browser. “Remember” opts into local storage. A shared link deliberately includes the inputs in a URL fragment; anyone given it can read them. No advisor uploads or analytics are used. Normal hosting access logs still exist.

Contribution room & existing savings

Room includes this year. Check the CRA assessment and your own current contribution records. Defaults assume no unused prior room and previous-year pay of $100,000. Existing balances do not tell us remaining room.

Returns, inflation & market shock

Presets are illustrative assumptions, not Vanguard forecasts. A shock is an extra fall at the start of the selected age, before new deposits; normal simulated returns continue afterward.

Transfers & optional managed fees

Direct registered transfers are not taxable withdrawals. Confirm permission, vesting, lock-in and any matching suspension with the employer. A managed-fee input is a cost sensitivity, not an actual Wealthsimple portfolio holding this ETF.
